**Discussion.**
- **Normal demand.** VANET-sensed control keeps almost all of the oracle gain from 80% penetration upward: waiting time falls 36-39% against fixed-time, the same as the oracle (38%). At 50% it keeps about half of the gain (19%). At 20% it is 28% *worse* than fixed-time. With one car in five reporting, each beacon is scaled ×5, so the estimate jumps between 0 and 5 vehicles (queue-estimate error 0.58 vehicles vs 0.06 at full penetration) and the controller switches on noise.
- **Congested demand.** VANET control beats fixed-time at every penetration (31-46%) and also beats the oracle (28%), even at 100% penetration (41%). This is not because less information is better. The two count queues differently: the oracle counts every halted vehicle on the whole 230 m approach, the RSU only vehicles below 1 m/s within 100 m of the junction. In heavy traffic the RSU count saturates, so the controller does not hold a long green for a queue that spills back beyond what one green can serve, and it rotates greens more evenly. The best queue definition is therefore part of the design, not just the sensing accuracy.
- **Estimate error** grows as penetration drops (0.06 to 0.58 vehicles in normal traffic, 0.28 to 1.13 in congestion) because fewer beacons are scaled by 1/penetration.

**Discussion.**
- **Preemption works over the VANET as well as with perfect knowledge.** Oracle preemption cuts the ambulance's travel time by 25.6% against adaptive control (87.6 s to 65.2 s). VANET preemption reaches 25-27% at every loss rate up to 50%, and no junction was ever missed (0 missed preemptions in all 90 VANET runs).
- **Packet loss barely matters** because an EV_ALERT is sent every 0.5 s and preemption starts about 18 s before arrival. Even at 50% loss dozens of alerts arrive in time. Only at 50% loss without relay does the result get worse and more variable (68.7 ± 13.0 s): in three seeds (6, 7, 9) the ambulance needed 76-96 s instead of 62-78 s with the relay, because fewer alerts got through in time. With the relay, 50% loss costs nothing (64.1 s).
- **The V2V relay** adds redundancy. With a 300 m radio range on a 250 m grid each RSU is usually in direct range, so relay on and off are equal up to 30% loss; the relay only pays off at very high loss.
- **Cost for other traffic is small:** average waiting time rises from 12.2 s (adaptive) to 12.5-13.1 s with preemption.
- **GA-tuned parameters** keep the ambulance gain (66.5 s) and cut everyone's waiting time to 9.2 s (24% better than adaptive). That gain most likely comes from shorter, more responsive greens (min_green 5 s, max_green 25 s, decision every 1 s), not from the preemption settings.

**Discussion.**

With every car following advice, stops per vehicle fall 40% (1.45 to 0.86), CO₂ and fuel 2.9%, and waiting time from 19.4 s to 13.5 s, with no increase in travel time. The effect grows steadily with the share of eco cars (stops -11%, -25%, -40% at 30%, 60%, 100%). About 20% of advice updates are "slow".
- **Congested demand.** Queues, not signal timing, decide when a car reaches the line, so the advisor gives "slow" advice half as often (about 9%). Stops still fall 10% at 100% eco cars; the CO₂ change (-1%) is not significant.

- **Stops vs CO₂.** Avoiding a stop saves little CO₂ in the HBEFA emission model: 40% fewer stops gave 2.9% less CO₂. The larger benefits are fewer stops and shorter waits.
- **Safety rules.** Cars that still need a lane change, or that are behind a queue within 60 m, get no speed advice; this removed the lane deadlocks seen in early tests.

**Discussion** (5 seeds per row, so a detection rate of 80% means 4 of 5 seeds).
- **Road closures** are detected in 80-100% of runs, about 33 s after they start (41 s at 20% penetration). Detection needs a stopped equipped vehicle for 30 s, so the delay is mostly that hold time. Closures add 12-20 s to the average trip. A\* navigation with the TMC incident map reduces the added delay at 50% and 100% penetration (12.0-12.7 s vs 14.2-15.4 s without navigation) but not at 20% (20.4 s vs 17.6 s).
- **Stopped vehicles** (one lane blocked) are detected only when penetration is high: 0% at 20%, 40% at 50%, 100% at full penetration. Detection relies on beacons from the stopped vehicle itself (the cars behind it change lanes and pass), so it is seen only if it is equipped. Navigating cars then avoid the blocked edge (0 entries), while about 30 non-navigating cars still drive onto it, which costs little because the other lane stays open (added delay about 1 s).
- **False flags** (vehicles reported as incidents that were not) stay below 0.6 per run.

## 6. GA tuning and whether the gain holds

**Target `signals`**: best tuning fitness 29.80 vs defaults 32.32 on tuning seeds [101, 102, 103]; tuned values `{"min_green": 5.0, "max_green": 24.9969, "queue_threshold": 4.0, "decision_interval": 1.0, "eta_threshold": 10.4461, "alert_interval": 0.3971, "beacon_interval": 0.5061}`.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| pen 0.5, loss 0.1 (tuning) | 34.144 ± 2.731 | 32.84 ± 2.241 | 3.82% | 6/10 | 0.193 |
| pen 0.8, loss 0.3 | 33.022 ± 1.923 | 32.533 ± 2.811 | 1.48% | 5/10 | 0.557 |

**Target `aco`**: best tuning fitness 115.63 vs defaults 175.86 on tuning seeds [101, 102, 103]; tuned values `{"alpha": 0.4945, "beta": 4.5038, "rho": 0.2096, "n_ants": 6.0, "n_iterations": 3.0, "Q": 132.6325, "elite_k": 3.0}`.

| Condition | Default fitness | Tuned fitness | Improvement | Seeds better | p-value |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| pen 0.5, loss 0.1 (tuning) | 182.356 ± 18.675 | 119.187 ± 5.997 | 34.64% | 10/10 | 0.002 |
| pen 0.8, loss 0.3 | 230.485 ± 26.246 | 128.111 ± 8.204 | 44.42% | 10/10 | 0.002 |

**Discussion.**
- **ACO.** Tuning generalises: on held-out seeds 1-10 the tuned parameters beat the defaults on every seed (p = 0.002), at the tuning condition (-35%) and at the other communication condition (-44%). Most of the gain is search cost: the GA chose 6 ants and 3 iterations (defaults: 10 and 10), which cuts route-search time about 3-4× while keeping travel times. The search penalty counts edge-cost evaluations, not milliseconds, so the result does not depend on how busy the computer was (method.md section 8.4).
- **Signals.** The held-out gain is small and not significant (3.8%, 6 of 10 seeds better, p = 0.19; 1.5% at the other condition). The fitness adds half the ambulance's travel time, which tuning hardly changes, so the waiting-time gain is diluted. In the full evaluation the tuned controller does cut waiting time clearly (9.2 s vs 12.5 s, emergency scenario).
- **Search limits.** Two signal genes ended at their lower bounds (min_green 5 s, decision_interval 1 s), so wider bounds might help. 12 generations × 12 chromosomes is a small search for 7 genes, so the result is an improvement over the defaults, not an optimum.

## 7. Limitations

- **Emulated channel.** The VANET is emulated in Python: distance-based loss, delay and jitter, but no MAC contention, interference or obstacles.
- **Small network.** The 2x2 grid offers few alternative routes, so navigation gains are small; results may not transfer to large networks.
- **A\* finds the same path as Dijkstra** for the same edge costs. Its benefit is fewer node expansions.
- **Model estimates.** Fuel and CO₂ come from SUMO's HBEFA4 emission model (petrol Euro 4 passenger car).
- **Simplified ambulances.** They obey red lights and other cars form no rescue lane, so preemption is what clears their way.
- **Tuning horizon.** GA fitness uses 600 s runs on three seeds; the evaluation uses 1000 s runs on ten held-out seeds.

## 8. Future work

- **Real maps.** Use OpenStreetMap networks with SUMO's netconvert, with more junctions and real route choice.
- **Full network simulator.** Run the communication part in OMNeT++/Veins instead of the emulated channel.
- **Reinforcement learning.** RL signal control that uses the same VANET StateProvider.
- **Commitment-based GLOSA.** Adaptive controllers that commit to their next switch a few seconds ahead, so eco-driving also works with adaptive signals.
- **Rescue lane.** Model how other vehicles make way for ambulances (SUMO bluelight device with the sublane model).
